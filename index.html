01 · Director's Note

Three distinct
aesthetics.

This exploration separates the colour palettes by aesthetic, not just hue — a different watercolour composition, energy and silver behaviour for each. They should look like three different paintings from the same studio.

01 — Atelier Lagoon. The sunlit, vibrant page. Cyan bloom, scattered silver flecks, light-handed and clean.

02 — Mineral Tide. The dramatic, ink-deep page. A diagonal jade tideline with a single silver vein where pigment meets paper.

03 — Overcast Celadon. The almost-empty page. A breath of celadon mist, a sparse silver dust. The quietest of the three.

Aesthetic Concept

The most legible read on the client's brief: water you can see your feet through. A single centred bloom that lets the wordmark sit in the brightest part of the page, framed by scatter and silver dust. The composition is symmetric on purpose — this palette is the calmest in geometry, the most vibrant in hue.

Watercolour Execution

Print. Two-plate process on coated cotton: PMS 3252 + PMS 5473 with a screen build for the bloom. The fleck is a third pass — PMS 8001 silver in fine confetti dots, hand-positioned per layout. Cotton paper is non-negotiable; cheap stock kills the bleed.

Digital. Stacked radial gradients with displacement-noise edges rendered as multiply layers. Silver flecks are real SVG circles with a radial metallic fill, not flat dots.

Silver & the 3-Ring Border

All three rings receive the foil — Lagoon's openness can carry the metallic weight. The rings sit in the lightest area of the wash, so the silver reads clearly against paper white without competing with the wordmark.

Loose silver flecks elsewhere on the page are decorative spill and may be omitted at small scales (e.g. appointment cards under 70 mm wide).

Aesthetic Concept

The dramatic page. Pigment runs corner to corner like a coastline at storm; the wordmark sits in the negative space the water hasn't reached. The most cinematic of the three palettes and the one that shows the silver to its best advantage — one vein, not confetti.

Watercolour Execution

Print. Seaweed inspired watercolour flooded with two-pass PMS 5483 build. The silver vein is a fine hot-foil strike — 0.4 pt minimum — registered to follow the dried edge.

Digital. A diagonal SVG path filled with a linear gradient, edge-displaced for organic dry. The silver vein is a single stroked path with the same displacement filter applied — so vein and watercolour share one geometry.

Silver & the 3-Ring Border

The 3-ring border is rendered tonally, not metallically. Outer ring in PMS 877 silver foil; the two inner rings drop to Slate Bathystem ink — the silver becomes the rim, not the whole mark.

The independent vein on the page reads as the metallic. The border and the vein speak to each other without doubling up.

Aesthetic Concept

The page that is mostly paper. A pulled-back celadon as a breath — never a statement. This is the option to choose if the brand should look most like a Japanese ceramics studio. Lowest energy, highest restraint, most generous use of negative space.

Watercolour Execution

Print. A 10–14% screen of Harbour Pine printed onto Colorplan Bone or Crush Tea Leaf. Tone-on-tone — the wash is felt, not seen. Silver dust is matte foil, kept under seven specks per A4.

Digital. A single low-amplitude SVG bloom at 24% maximum opacity. Animations and parallax are off-limits — the wash must feel weather, not motion.

Silver & the 3-Ring Border

The quietest silver of the three. Matte foil on the outer ring only; the two inner rings step down to a 60% tint of Driftwood Graphite, introducing a third tonal interval so the silver doesn't shout.

On screen, silver is a flat muted gradient (#C9C8C3 → #9A9994) at 1 px stroke — no animated shimmer.

06 · Appendix

One silver,
four finishes.

The client leans toward silver on the 3-ring border. We recommend specifying silver as a material, not a colour — the same Pantone struck in different finishes reads differently across surfaces. Each palette has a different default finish, summarised below.

The 3-ring border always retains the locked organic geometry — the silver only changes how the rings catch light.
